Screenator 2.0
==============

Screenator is a vibe-coded screen annotation tool for X11, developed by
Mahmoud Alnaanah <malnaanah@gmail.com>.  It is based on Gromit-MPX 1.4.2
and keeps the upstream GPL-2.0 license and attribution.

Packages:
  - screenator 2.0 (amd64)
  - Screenator-2.0-x86_64.AppImage

Layout:
  screenator/           Source code, data files, documentation and build script.
  screenator/src/       C sources (screenator.c/h, callbacks, config, input).
  screenator/data/      Icons, cursors, default tool configuration, desktop and
                        AppStream metadata.
  screenator/build.sh   Builds the .deb and the AppImage.
  screenator/CMakeLists.txt
                        CMake build definition.
  build/                Built packages:
                          screenator_2.0_amd64.deb
                          Screenator-2.0-x86_64.AppImage
  info.txt              This file.

Features:
  - Pen, marker, arrow, text, shape and eraser tools.
  - The Shapes submenu holds the Marker, Free Line, Spline, Polygon, Triangle,
    Arrow, Angled Line, Angled Arrow, Rectangle, Ellipse, Rounded Rectangle and
    Paint Bucket tools.  Shapes are drawn by dragging; Free Line and Arrow follow the
    drag direction, while Angled Line and Angled Arrow snap to 0, 30, 45, 60,
    90, 120, 135 and 150 degrees.  Arrow and Angled Arrow add an arrowhead at
    the end point.  Spline is placed click by click: each click adds a node, the
    nodes are joined by a smooth Bezier curve, and dragging a node moves it.
    Every node has two round Bezier handles: dragging one mirrors the other so
    the curve stays smooth, and holding Ctrl while dragging moves only that
    side to give the node a corner.  Click the last node again, double-click or
    right-click to finish the curve.
    Polygon is placed with a single click and stays editable with four handles:
    the square handle controls the number of sides from 3 to 16 (pressing it
    resets the shape to 3 sides; moving away from the handle in any direction
    adds sides and moving back towards it removes them, down to 3 at the
    handle), one round handle sets the radius, one sets the rotation, and one
    moves the inner vertices outwards until the shape becomes a regular
    polygon.  Drag inside the shape to move it; clicking empty space commits it
    and places the next one, and a double-click or right-click commits it.
    Triangle is placed with a single click and keeps three round vertex
    handles, so it can be stretched and skewed freely; drag inside it to move
    it, click empty space to commit it and place the next one, and
    double-click or right-click to commit it.  The Rounded Rectangle keeps a
    roundness handle on its top edge after the drag: moving it away from the
    corner rounds the corners more and moving it back makes them sharper.
    Paint Bucket fills the closed area under the click with the Shapes Fill
    Color; an area that reaches the screen edge is treated as not closed and is
    left unchanged.
  - The Marker draws a yellow highlighter stroke with a fixed 32 px width and
    50% opacity.  It keeps its own settings, so the global colour, thickness
    and opacity are not changed when it is selected.
  - A Shapes Fill Color submenu fills rectangles, ellipses, rounded
    rectangles, polygons and triangles with one of the line colours, or leaves
    them unfilled (the default).  After None, the colours are listed Green, Blue,
    Yellow, Black, White, Red; Green is the default fill, so the Paint Bucket
    fills green out of the box.  The angled line is never filled.
  - The Text tool shows an I-beam cursor and lets you write directly on the
    screen: click where the text should start and type in the small inline
    editor that appears there.  The text uses the pen colour and the selected
    font; Enter places it and Escape cancels.  Right-to-left text such as
    Arabic extends to the left of the click position.
  - A small draggable floating icon.  Left or right click opens the menu.
  - The same menu is available from the system tray icon.
  - Selecting a tool shows a short hint about the mouse buttons, with a
    "Never show this message again" option.  The Spline hint also explains
    how to move its handles and has its own option.
    Reset Settings keeps both choices.
  - Line colour, line thickness, eraser size, opacity, text font, shape fill
    and floating icon size can be changed from the menu and are remembered
    across restarts.  The eraser cursor is drawn as a circle that matches the
    selected eraser size.
  - Pen strokes are smoothed with curved segments, and motion event
    compression is disabled so fast strokes stay smooth.
  - Reset Settings restores red, 6 px, 100% opacity, no shape fill, Arial Bold
    24, a 40 px eraser and a 48x48 icon.
  - Undo/redo, clear screen, show/hide drawing and show/hide floating icon.
  - Hotkeys: F9 toggles painting, SHIFT-F9 clears, CTRL-F9 toggles visibility,
    ALT-F9 quits, F8 undoes and SHIFT-F8 redoes.  While painting mode is
    active, Ctrl+Z also undoes and Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes.
  - Multi-pointer support inherited from Gromit-MPX.

Configuration:
  ~/.config/screenator.ini          Menu settings (tool, colour, width, eraser
                                    width, opacity, shape fill, text font, icon
                                    size, hint choices).
  ~/.config/screenator.cfg          User tool configuration.
  /etc/screenator/screenator.cfg    System-wide tool configuration, used when
                                    the user configuration does not exist.

  Inside the AppImage, AppRun exports SCREENATOR_CFG and SCREENATOR_DATADIR so the
  bundled configuration and icons are found even though the AppImage is
  mounted at a temporary path.

Differences from Gromit-MPX 1.4.2:
  - The application, files, identifiers, configuration paths, desktop entry,
    icons and X11 selection atoms were renamed to Screenator, and the version
    was changed to 2.0.
  - Unused code was removed: the introduction assistant, the support/donation
    handlers, Toggle Painting-all, Thicker/Thinner lines, the line_thickener
    pressure offset, and their prototypes, keys and configuration entries.
  - Width, colour and opacity from the menu are applied to every pen tool,
    including the tools defined in screenator.cfg.  The Line Thickness submenu
    marks the entry closest to the current width.
  - A Text tool with an I-beam cursor and a Change Font dialog were added.
    Text is typed directly on screen at the click position in the pen colour
    and the chosen Pango font; Enter places it and Escape cancels.  The font is
    saved with the other menu settings.
  - A Shapes submenu was added with Free Line, Spline, Polygon, Triangle,
    Arrow, Angled Line, Angled Arrow, Rectangle, Ellipse, Rounded Rectangle and
    Paint Bucket tools.  Shapes are drag-drawn; the free line and the arrow follow the drag
    direction, while the angled line and angled arrow snap to fixed angles.
    The arrow tools draw an arrowhead at the end point.  The spline is placed
    click by click: each click adds a node, the nodes are joined by a smooth
    Bezier curve, and dragging a node moves it.  Each node has two round Bezier
    handles; dragging one mirrors the other so the curve stays smooth, and
    holding Ctrl while dragging moves only that side to create a corner.
    Click the last node again, double-click or right-click to finish the curve.
    The polygon is placed with a single click and has four handles for its
    number of sides (3 to 16), radius, rotation and inner radius; pressing the
    side handle resets the shape to 3 sides, moving away from the handle adds
    sides and moving back towards it removes them, while the inner radius turns
    the shape into a regular polygon as it grows.  Drag inside the shape to
    move it, click empty space to commit it and place the next one, and
    double-click or right-click to commit it.
    The triangle is placed with a single click and has a round handle on each
    of its three vertices; dragging a handle moves that vertex, so the shape
    can be stretched and skewed.  Drag inside it to move it, click empty space
    to commit it and place the next one, and double-click or right-click to
    commit it.  The rounded rectangle keeps a roundness handle on its top edge
    after the drag, which rounds the corners more when moved away from the
    corner and makes them sharper when moved back.
    The paint bucket fills the closed area under the click with the shape fill
    colour and shows a bucket cursor; a region that reaches the screen edge is
    treated as not closed and is left unchanged.
    Rectangles, ellipses, rounded rectangles, polygons and triangles can
    optionally be filled with a shape fill colour, which is saved with the
    other menu settings.
  - A Marker tool was added to the Shapes submenu.  It draws a translucent
    yellow highlighter stroke with a fixed 32 px width and does not change the
    global colour, thickness or opacity.
  - The eraser has its own 20/40/60/120 px size menu and a circular cursor that
    matches the erasing area.  The default font is Arial Bold, menu icons are
    scaled to 16 px, the Shapes submenu sits below Eraser, Undo/Redo sit above
    Change Font, and pen strokes are smoothed with curved segments.
  - While painting mode is active, Ctrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes; the
    keys are grabbed only while painting, so they keep their normal meaning in
    other applications.
  - The About dialog names the author and email, and states that Screenator is
    a vibe-coded screen annotation app based on Gromit-MPX 1.4.2.

Build:
  cd screenator
  ./build.sh

  The script downloads and stages the development headers under
  /tmp/opencode/screenator-build-stage (no root required), compiles with CMake,
  builds the .deb, and then builds the AppImage with linuxdeploy, the
  linuxdeploy GTK plugin and appimagetool.  The tools are cached in
  /tmp/opencode/screenator-appimage-tools.

  Both outputs are written to build/ at the project root.  The packages are
  not installed and the GUI is not tested by the build script.
