Screenator 8.0
==============

Screenator is a vibe-coded screen annotation tool for X11, developed by
Mahmoud Alnaanah <malnaanah@gmail.com>.  It is based on Gromit-MPX 1.4.2
and keeps the upstream GPL-2.0 license and attribution.

Packages:
  - screenator 8.0 (amd64) .deb
  - screenator 8.0 (x86_64) .rpm
  - Screenator-8.0-x86_64.AppImage
  - Screenator-7.0-win64.zip (Windows port, still 7.0, built separately)

Features:
  - Pen, marker, arrow, text, shape and eraser tools.
  - The Settings dialog has a General tab (Save Annotations on Exit, Change
    Font, Show Settings Indicator, Timer Delay, and Reset app settings and
    drawings), a Paper tab (continuous scrolling and paper size), a Toolbar
    tab (direction, icon size, each icon's visibility and order, changed by
    dragging), a Floating Icon tab (icon size and position)
    and a Shortcuts tab (every editable shortcut).  The tool bar is horizontal
    on the top edge by default; choosing Vertical places it on the right edge.
    The tool bar and floating icon are visible by default.  Actions sits below
    Shapes in the
    main
    menu and holds Switch Mode, Undo, Redo, Clear Screen, Toggle Visibility,
    Screenshot, Show toolbar (F10), Show Floating Icon (F9) and Show Hints.
    The floating icon and tool bar stay clickable while a tool is selected;
    hovering them shows the normal pointer and suspends drawing.
    With Save
    Annotations on Exit on (the default), the drawings are saved to
    ~/.config/Screenator/screenator-annotations.png when Screenator quits and restored on
    the next start; changed papers are offered for saving on exit, and Clear
    Screen removes the saved drawing.  The Save toolbar button, directly before
    Paper Type, saves only the current changed paper to Documents or takes a
    screenshot in Screen mode.
  - The Timer tool (F12) is available in Screen Annotation mode only. It
    releases the input for the Timer Delay set in
    Settings > General, so you can switch to another application.  Other tools
    cannot be selected during the countdown.  When it ends, the Pen tool
    becomes active.  If the pointer grab is blocked when
    the timer ends, for example by another application's context menu,
    Screenator freezes the current screen as a temporary background so the
    tool can draw over it; exiting the tool removes the frozen background.
  - The Arrow tool sits directly after the Eraser.  The Shapes submenu holds
    the Marker, Line, Spline, Polygon, Triangle, Ellipse, Rounded Rectangle,
    Paint Bucket, Math Function and Parallel Lines tools.  Shapes are drawn by
    dragging; the Line and Arrow follow the
    drag direction, which snaps to 0, 30, 45, 60, 90 and so on while Ctrl is
    held, and follows the pointer without it.
    The Arrow adds a triangular arrowhead at the end point by default.  The
    head grows with the line thickness and stays easy to see even with a thin
    line.  A square handle offset from the middle of the line changes the
    heads: pressing it resets to none and dragging away cycles start, end and
    both.  A placed line or arrow stays editable: each end point has a round
    handle, and dragging the line itself moves the whole shape; clicking
    outside, double-clicking or right-clicking commits it.  Spline is placed click by click: each click adds a node, the
    nodes are joined by a smooth Bezier curve, and dragging a node moves it.
    Every node has two round Bezier handles: dragging one mirrors the other so
    the curve stays smooth, and holding Ctrl while dragging moves only that
    side to give the node a corner.  Click the last node again, double-click or
    right-click to finish the curve.
    Polygon: the first click sets its centre, and dragging sets the radius and
    rotation around that centre; a plain click keeps a default size.  The
    shape stays editable with four handles: the square handle controls the
    number of sides from 3 to 16 (pressing it resets the shape to 3 sides;
    moving away from the handle in any direction adds sides and moving back
    towards it removes them, down to 3 at the handle), one round handle sets
    the radius, one sets the rotation, and one moves the inner vertices
    outwards until the shape becomes a regular polygon; that handle snaps to
    the flat edge when it gets close, so a regular polygon is easy to hit.
    Drag inside the shape to move it; clicking empty space commits it and
    places the next one, and a double-click or right-click commits it.
    Triangle: each click places the next vertex.  A line follows the pointer
    after the first click, and the remaining sides follow after the second
    click.  Dragging does not place a point; the line keeps following the
    pointer until the next click.  The third click fixes the last vertex and
    closes the triangle.  The triangle keeps three round vertex handles, so
    it can be stretched and skewed freely; holding Ctrl while dragging a
    vertex snaps the angle at that vertex to 30, 45, 60 or 90 degrees.  Drag
    inside it to move it, click empty space to commit it and place the next
    one, and double-click or right-click to commit it.
    The outlines of these closed shapes are drawn outside their border, so a
    thick stroke never covers the inside of the shape.
    The Ellipse and Rounded Rectangle stay editable after the drag.  Every
    placed shape keeps a square handle on each of its four corners and a round
    rotation handle above it, and dragging inside the shape moves it; rotation
    is free, and holding Ctrl snaps it to 0, 30, 45, 60, 90 and so on.
    The Rounded Rectangle also keeps a roundness handle on its top edge: moving
    it away from the corner rounds the corners more and moving it back makes
    them sharper.  Clicking outside the shape, double-clicking or right-clicking
    commits it.
    Math Function opens a dialog for the expression and the range.  The
    Rectangular mode plots y = f(x) over an x range and takes a function of x;
    the Polar mode plots r = f(theta) over a theta range, where theta is
    reserved for the angle and the default range is 0 to 2*pi, a full circle.
    pi and e are the constants pi and Euler's number, and multiplication can be
    implied (2pi*x).  A preset drop-down fills the expression with
    cos(2*pi*x), sin(2*pi*x), unit(x), delta(x), rect(x), e^(x), log(x),
    1/x, sin(2*pi*x)/(2*pi*x) or (cos(2*pi*x)+2)*cos(20*pi*x); in polar mode
    the same presets use theta instead of x.  unit(x) is the unit step,
    delta(x) is the Dirac delta, drawn as an arrow where its argument is zero,
    and rect(x) is a rectangle pulse that is 1 for |x| <= 1/2 and 0 elsewhere.
    The defaults are cos(2*pi*x) from x = -2 to x = 2 and cos(3*theta) from
    theta = 0 to 2*pi; each mode keeps its own expression and range.  The plot
    is drawn inside a frame that is dragged out like the other shapes and is
    scaled to fill it, so the corner handles also set its amplitude; polar plots
    use one scale for both axes, so the curve stays
    round in a non-square frame.  The plot can be moved, resized and rotated
    with the usual handles.  Selecting the tool from the Shapes menu asks for
    the expression first, and choosing it again changes the expression; a plot
    that is still editable updates at once.
    A point where the expression is undefined is filled in with its limit when
    both sides agree, so sin(x)/x is drawn through x = 0; a real pole such as
    1/x or tan(x) breaks the curve instead.
    Parallel Lines draws between 2 and 40 evenly spaced vertical lines with the
    current line thickness.  The square handle outside the left edge sets their
    number: pressing it resets the shape to two lines and moving away adds
    more.  The square handle outside the right edge changes the arrowheads on
    the lines: pressing it resets to none and moving away adds them in the
    order top, bottom and both; the handle shows the current mode.  The lines
    can be moved, resized and rotated with the usual handles.
    Random Signal draws a random waveform.  A single click places a
    default-size signal centred on the click; dragging out a frame sets its
    size.  The square handle outside the left edge resets the smoothness to 1
    (sharpest); dragging it away in any direction raises it up to 16
    (smoothest).  The default is 5.  Clicking the signal without dragging
    generates a new signal with a new seed.  The frame can be moved, resized
    and rotated with the usual handles.
    Paint Bucket fills the closed area under the click with the Shapes Fill
    Color; an area that reaches the screen edge is treated as not closed and is
    left unchanged.  With None selected the bucket erases the enclosed fill
    instead, leaving the strokes around it.
  - The Marker draws a highlighter stroke with a fixed 32 px width, using the
    current line colour.  Its opacity is 40% by default and is independent of
    the global pen opacity.  The Marker Opacity menu with 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%,
    50%, 60%, 70%, 80% and 90% is available from the app menu and tool bar; the
    choice is remembered across restarts.  Selecting the Marker
    switches the line colour to yellow; leaving the Marker or exiting painting
    restores the colour that was active before.  The mouse wheel or the stylus
    first side button changes its colour.
  - Line Thickness offers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 12, 24 and 48 pixels; the menu marks the
    entry closest to the current width.
  - Opacity offers 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, 50%, 60%, 70%, 80% and 90%.
  - A Shapes Fill Color submenu fills ellipses, rounded rectangles, polygons
    and triangles with one of the line colours, or leaves them unfilled (the
    default).  After None, the colours are listed Green, Blue, Yellow, Black,
    White, Red, Orange and Gray; Green is the default fill, so the Paint Bucket
    fills green out of the box.  Lines and arrows are never filled.  Line
    Color and Shapes Fill Color also have a Custom... entry that opens a
    colour chooser; the custom colour starts as magenta and is remembered.
  - The Text tool shows an I-beam cursor and lets you write directly on the
    screen: click where the text should start and type in the small inline
    editor that appears there.  The text uses the pen colour and the selected
    font and is centred vertically on the click point; Enter places it and
    Escape cancels.  While the editor is open the mouse buttons are disabled, so
    a click cannot leave painting mode or reach the application below; Enter or
    Escape enables them again.  The text and its blinking caret are drawn above
    the annotations and the paper page, so they stay visible even over a
    drawing.  The middle mouse button still erases while it is held, also with
    the Text tool selected.  Right-to-left
    text such as Arabic extends to the left of the click position.  A placed
    text stays editable: drag it to move it, drag a corner handle to change
    the font size and drag its round handle to rotate it.
  - A small draggable floating icon.  Left or right click opens the menu.  The
    icon switches to its active (red) variant while a painting mode is on.
  - The settings indicator is a ring around the floating icon's circle.  The
    inner white ring is divided into nine equal sectors, one for each line
    thickness (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 12, 24 and 48 pixels), each with a one pixel black
    stroke.  Starting at angle 0 and going counter clockwise, the sectors up
    to and including the selected thickness are filled magenta.  The outer
    six pixel ring has the line colour in its upper half and the shape fill
    colour in its lower half; when shapes are not filled, the lower half shows twelve
    black and white radial stripes.  Both halves have a one pixel black
    stroke.  Left or right click on the ring opens the same menu as the
    floating icon.  "Show Settings Indicator" in Settings turns it off and on;
    it is on by default and is disabled while the floating icon is hidden.
  - The same menu is available from the system tray icon.
  - Dialogs and message boxes, such as the Math Function dialog and the hints
    dialog, are shown above the drawings and the paper page, so both stay
    visible behind them.  Every dialog has a title bar with a close button and
    a visible border, and each dialog is raised as it opens, which also works
    in virtual machines without a compositor.
  - A Paper submenu holds the paper tools, and Actions holds Switch Mode (F3),
    Undo, Redo, Clear Screen, Toggle Visibility, Screenshot, Show toolbar
    (F10), Show Floating Icon (F9) and Show Hints.  Switch Mode is also the
    first item in the toolbar.  Plain, Ruled and
    Graph are
    white pages (with blue writing lines for Ruled and a grey square grid for
    Graph), and selecting PDF opens the desktop's own file chooser (KDE,
    GNOME, etc.).  Reset/Open PDF. in the Paper menu or the tool bar's Paper
    Type menu resets the current Plain, Ruled or Graph paper to one empty page,
    or opens another PDF when the current paper is PDF.  If the current paper
    has unsaved changes, Screenator asks whether to save it in the Documents
    folder first.  Selecting a paper type, or opening a PDF, switches to
    Paper mode automatically and brings the Paper window to the front.
    Switching between Screen and Paper always exits the current tool and leaves
    Cursor selected.  The same applies when selecting a paper type, resetting a
    paper or opening a PDF.  Every type keeps its own pages, drawings and
    loaded PDF while another type is shown, so switching back restores them
    without a save prompt.  Switching paper types keeps the active tool.  The selected paper type and PDF file are remembered
    across restarts.  Each PDF file remembers the page it was last closed on
    when reopened from Screenator or the desktop.  Paper saves
    show a progress overlay until the PDF is complete, then the saved path.
    Saving a screenshot shows a confirmation with the saved path.  Opening a PDF with
    Screenator (Open With or "screenator file.pdf") starts directly in Paper
    mode with that PDF; if an instance is already running, the PDF is
    forwarded to it.  The desktop entry registers Screenator as a PDF
    handler.  While Paper mode is active, the page is shown in a normal
    managed window, not a fullscreen overlay: other applications and dialogs
    can cover it, and Alt+Tab switches away normally.  It takes the mouse and
    keyboard while focused, and stays when Clear Screen is selected.  Ctrl+W
    switches to Window mode: the Paper window becomes a normal resizable
    window, the paper page width fits the window automatically, and the desktop
    panel can appear above it.  The drawing tool and its cursor are active only
    inside the Paper window, and no global pointer grab is taken, so resizing,
    the window frame and other applications keep working normally.  Ctrl/Alt
    shortcuts are received by the focused Paper window as ordinary key events
    instead of being grabbed globally.  Press it again to return to fullscreen.
    Ctrl+D leaves fullscreen into a borderless, non-resizable window covering
    the monitor, so the desktop panel stays above the page; press it again to
    return to fullscreen.  In every Paper presentation (fullscreen, Ctrl+D and
    Window mode), the drawing tool and cursor are limited to the Paper window,
    and Ctrl/Alt shortcuts are received only while that window is focused.
    Ctrl+S, or the Save toolbar button,
    saves only the current paper if it has changed, automatically to the
    Documents folder as Screenator-plain-YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS.pdf,
    Screenator-ruled-..., Screenator-graph-..., or Screenator-file-... for
    opened PDF files (with a numeric suffix if that name exists), and shows
    the saved path.  Drawings are vector paths, text is saved as text, and the
    original PDF pages and paper backgrounds stay vector.  Undo and redo keep
    drawings and text vector as well.  Eraser strokes over empty space leave the
    vector layer untouched; when ink is erased, the remaining drawings stay
    vector and text stays text.  On exit Screenator
    offers once to save every changed
    paper; switching paper types never shows a save prompt.  Ctrl+B and Ctrl+A
    insert a page before or after the current page in
    Plain, Ruled and Graph.  With Continuous scrolling on (the default), the
    selected paper is shown as one continuous vertical document with a grey
    gap between its pages, and Up/Down move smoothly through all of them.
    Page Up/Page Down move to the previous or next page and Home/End jump to
    the first or last page.  With it off, pages are shown one at a time;
    scrolling moves within the
    current page and moves to the next or previous page at the top or bottom
    edge.  In Paper mode, holding the mouse middle
    button or stylus side button 1 pans the paper and shows a hand cursor;
    holding the right mouse button or stylus side button 2 selects the Eraser
    and shows the eraser cursor.  No long press is needed, and both work
    whether a tool is active or not; releasing the right button or stylus
    button 2 restores the previous tool.  A right click or stylus side button
    2 acts as Escape while a shape or text is unfinished; after it is
    committed, the same button is a temporary eraser and keeps the selected
    tool.  The mouse wheel scrolls the pages, except while a placed shape or
    text shows its handles: then the wheel and stylus button 1 change its
    colour.  While text is being typed, the wheel and stylus side button 1
    change the text colour.  Right click, stylus side button 2 or mouse
    button 2 accepts the text like Enter.
    F10 shows or hides the tool bar in both Screen and Paper modes.  It is
    horizontal on the top edge by default; Settings > General can place it
    vertically on the right edge, and Settings > Toolbar chooses the visible
    icons and their order.  It contains Switch Mode first, then Cursor, Pen,
    Text, Eraser, Arrow, Timer, Shapes, Undo, Redo, Save, Paper Type, Insert
    Page Before, Delete Page, Insert Page After, Current Page, Go to Page,
    Clear Screen, Toggle Visibility, Eraser
    Size, Line Color, Thickness, Fill Color, Opacity, Marker Opacity,
    Settings, Show Hints and Quit last.  The Timer icon appears only in Screen mode, and
    Toggle Visibility is hidden from the tool bar in Paper mode.  Paper Type
    selects a paper type; the insert-page
    buttons insert a
    page in an active Plain, Ruled or Graph paper and are hidden in Screen
    mode.  Save saves the current changed paper to Documents or takes a
    screenshot in Screen mode.  The selected tool is
    highlighted.  Drag any icon or the gaps between them to move the bar; a
    short click still selects the tool.  Cursor exits the current tool and is
    available in both modes.  In Paper mode with no active tool, the middle
    mouse button or stylus side button 1 pans, the wheel scrolls, and the
    right button or stylus side button 2 erases.  The stylus
    tip.  The bar
    stays open when a tool or a menu entry is chosen, its icons show tooltips,
    its direction and icon size (16 to 64 px) are set in Settings > General,
    its visible icons and order are set in Settings > Toolbar, its shortcuts
    are set in Settings > Shortcuts, and its open/closed state is remembered.
    Settings > Paper also
    sets continuous scrolling and the paper size: Screen, A4 portrait, A4
    landscape, or Custom width and height in millimetres.  The size applies to
    Plain, Ruled and Graph pages and scales their existing drawings; PDF pages
    keep the PDF's own size.  Continuous PDF pages are separated by a grey gap
    so their edges stay visible.  Screenator always starts on the normal
    desktop; unsaved paper pages are not restored.
  - A single, colourful Hints dialog appears when Screenator starts.  It is a
    700x500 scrollable column that summarises the common mouse and stylus
    controls plus Shape Editing tips, the global F9 shortcut and the Screen
    and Paper mode shortcuts in one place instead of repeating them for
    every tool.  It has a "Never show hints again" option beside the Close
    button, which is remembered across restarts.
    "Show Hints" in the menu opens the dialog again, and Reset app settings
    and drawings keeps the choice.  The hints text is loaded from the installed
    HTML file each time the dialog opens; it is not edited after installation.
    Headings, paragraphs, lists, bold/italic/underline text, links and coloured
    spans are supported.  Simple <style> rules such as h2 { color: #008800; }
    and span { color: #0000aa; } also work.
  - Line colour, line thickness, eraser size, opacity, text font, shape fill
    and floating icon size can be changed from the menu and are remembered
    across restarts, together with whether the floating icon and the settings
    indicator are shown.  The floating icon size is 16x16, 32x32, 48x48,
    64x64, 128x128 or 256x256 (48x48 by default); the settings ring doubles in
    width at 128x128 and triples at 256x256.  The eraser cursor is drawn as a
    circle that matches the selected eraser size.
  - While painting mode is active, the mouse wheel changes the setting selected
    through the line colours, including the custom colour, or the shape fill
    colours over the Paint Bucket.  With the Eraser tool selected, the wheel
    changes the eraser size instead.  There is no long-right-click menu; a
    right click finishes the current shape or exits painting like Escape.  The change is the same as
    picking it from the menu, so it is kept when another tool is selected and
    is shown in the settings indicator and the menus.
  - Pen strokes are smoothed with curved segments, and motion event
    compression is disabled so fast strokes stay smooth.
  - Reset app settings and drawings deletes the configuration file and saved
    drawings, clears every paper page, and restores red, 6 px, 100% opacity,
    no shape fill, Arial Bold 24, a 60 px eraser, a 48x48 icon, a 2 s Timer
    Delay and a visible 32 px horizontal tool bar with all icons.
  - Undo/redo, clear screen, show/hide drawing, show/hide floating icon,
    show hints and screenshots are in the Actions submenu.
  - F9 is a global shortcut that shows or hides the floating icon at any time.
    F8 selects the Pen tool, F6 the Arrow tool, F7 the Text tool and F12
    starts the Timer.  F10 shows or hides the tool bar in both modes; it is
    horizontal on the top edge by default, and Settings > General can make it
    vertical on the right edge.  F3 activates the selected paper or turns it
    off.  F4 saves a screenshot of the current screen, including the
    annotations or paper page, to the Pictures directory as
    Screenator-YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS.png in both Screen and Paper modes.  The old
    Gromit-MPX keys (Shift+F9, Ctrl+F9, Alt+F9, Shift+F8 and the old F8 undo)
    are no longer grabbed.  While painting mode
    is active, Ctrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes.  Esc first finishes the
    current shape or closes its handles and keeps the selected tool; press it
    again to exit the tool and select the Cursor tool, so its icon becomes
    active.
  - Multi-pointer support inherited from Gromit-MPX.
  - Stylus support: IMPORTANT: In your stylus configuration, set button 1
    (near the tip) to Middle Click and button 2 to Right Click.  The tip draws
    like the left mouse button, using the
    selected line width; pen pressure is ignored so it draws exactly like the
    mouse.  The first side button and the middle mouse button activate the
    temporary eraser on a normal press, with no long press.  While a text or
    shape still shows its handles, the first side button changes its colour
    instead.  A short click of the second side button acts like Escape/right
    click, finishing the shape being edited and keeping the tool active, or
    leaving painting mode when no shape is being edited.  There is no
    side-button popup menu.  In Paper mode the buttons change roles: hold side
    button 1 or the middle mouse button to pan, and hold side button 2 or the
    right mouse button to erase.  No long press is needed; releasing the right button or side button
    2 brings the previous tool back.  While a shape or text is unfinished,
    side button 2 acts as Escape.  The mouse wheel scrolls the pages.  The
    hints
    dialog covers the stylus controls at startup and can be reopened with
    "Show Hints".

Configuration:
  ~/.config/Screenator/screenator.ini          Menu settings (tool, colour, width, eraser
                                    width, opacity, shape fill, Paper mode,
                                    text font, icon size, math expression and
                                    range, hints choice).
  ~/.config/Screenator/screenator.cfg          User tool configuration.
  /etc/screenator/screenator.cfg    System-wide tool configuration, used when
                                    the user configuration does not exist.

  Inside the AppImage, AppRun exports SCREENATOR_CFG and SCREENATOR_DATADIR so the
  bundled configuration and icons are found even though the AppImage is
  mounted at a temporary path.

Differences from Gromit-MPX 1.4.2:
  - The application, files, identifiers, configuration paths, desktop entry,
    icons and X11 selection atoms were renamed to Screenator, and the version
    was changed to 4.0.
  - Unused code was removed: the introduction assistant, the support/donation
    handlers, Toggle Painting-all, Thicker/Thinner lines, the line_thickener
    pressure offset, and their prototypes, keys and configuration entries.
  - Width, colour and opacity from the menu are applied to every pen tool,
    including the tools defined in screenator.cfg.  The Line Thickness submenu
    marks the entry closest to the current width.
  - A Text tool with an I-beam cursor and a Change Font dialog were added.
    Text is typed directly on screen at the click position in the pen colour
    and the chosen Pango font; Enter places it and Escape cancels.  The mouse
    buttons are disabled while the inline editor is open and enabled again by
    Enter or Escape.  The font is saved with the other menu settings.
  - A Shapes submenu was added with Line, Spline, Polygon, Triangle, Ellipse,
    Rounded Rectangle and Paint Bucket tools; the Arrow tool is directly after
    the Eraser.  Shapes are drag-drawn;
    the line and the arrow follow the drag direction, which snaps to 0, 30,
    45, 60, 90 and so on while Ctrl is held.  The
    arrow draws an arrowhead at the end point.  A placed line or arrow keeps a
    round handle on each end point, and dragging the line moves the whole
    shape.  The spline is placed
    click by click: each click adds a node, the nodes are joined by a smooth
    Bezier curve, and dragging a node moves it.  Each node has two round Bezier
    handles; dragging one mirrors the other so the curve stays smooth, and
    holding Ctrl while dragging moves only that side to create a corner.
    Click the last node again, double-click or right-click to finish the curve.
    The polygon is placed with a single click and has four handles for its
    number of sides (3 to 16), radius, rotation and inner radius; pressing the
    side handle resets the shape to 3 sides, moving away from the handle adds
    sides and moving back towards it removes them, while the inner radius turns
    the shape into a regular polygon as it grows and snaps to the flat edge
    when the handle gets close.  Drag inside the shape to move it, click empty
    space to commit it and place the next one, and double-click or right-click
    to commit it.
    The triangle is placed with a single click and has a round handle on each
    of its three vertices; dragging a handle moves that vertex, so the shape
    can be stretched and skewed, and holding Ctrl snaps the angle at the
    dragged vertex to 30, 45, 60 or 90 degrees.  Drag inside it to move it,
    click empty space to commit it and place the next one, and double-click or
    right-click to commit it.  The rounded rectangle keeps a roundness handle on its top edge
    after the drag, which rounds the corners more when moved away from the
    corner and makes them sharper when moved back.  The ellipse and rounded
    rectangle stay editable after the drag: each keeps a square size handle on
    each of its four corners and a round rotation handle above it, dragging
    inside moves it, and holding Ctrl snaps the rotation to 0, 30, 45, 60, 90
    and so on.
    The text tool's placed text is editable in the same way, with its size
    handle changing the font size.
    The paint bucket fills the closed area under the click with the shape fill
    colour and shows a bucket cursor; a region that reaches the screen edge is
    treated as not closed and is left unchanged.
    The Math Function tool asks for an expression and a range in a dialog.
    The Rectangular mode plots y = f(x) and takes a function of x; the Polar
    mode plots r = f(theta), reserves theta for the angle and defaults to the
    full circle from 0 to 2*pi.  pi and e are the constants pi and Euler's
    number, implied multiplication is accepted, and the defaults are
    cos(2*pi*x) from -2 to 2 and cos(3*theta) from 0 to 2*pi.  A preset
    drop-down offers cos(2*pi*x), sin(2*pi*x), unit(x), delta(x), rect(x),
    e^(x), log(x), 1/x, sin(2*pi*x)/(2*pi*x) and
    (cos(2*pi*x)+2)*cos(20*pi*x), with theta in place of x in polar mode.
    unit(x) is the unit step, delta(x) is the Dirac delta, drawn as an arrow
    where its argument is zero, and rect(x) is a rectangle pulse that is 1 for
    |x| <= 1/2 and 0 elsewhere.  Each mode keeps its own expression and range.
    The plot is scaled to fill the frame (polar plots keep the curve round in a
    non-square frame), and an editable plot follows a new expression
    immediately.  The Parallel Lines tool draws 2 to 40 evenly spaced vertical
    lines with the current line thickness, a square handle outside the left
    edge sets their number, and dragging a square handle outside the right edge
    changes the arrowheads on the lines (none, top, bottom or both).  Both
    tools use the usual move, size and rotation handles.  A Random Signal tool
    draws a random waveform; its left handle sets the smoothness from 1
    (sharpest) to 10 (smoothest), and clicking the signal rolls a new one.
    Ellipses, rounded rectangles, polygons and triangles can optionally be
    filled with a shape fill colour, which is saved with the other menu
    settings.
  - A Marker tool was added to the Shapes submenu.  It draws a translucent
    yellow highlighter stroke with a fixed 32 px width and does not change the
    global colour, thickness or opacity.
  - The eraser has its own 20/40/60/120 px size menu and a circular cursor that
    matches the erasing area.  The default font is Arial Bold, menu icons are
    scaled to 16 px, the Shapes submenu sits below Eraser, Undo/Redo sit above
    Change Font, and pen strokes are smoothed with curved segments.
  - While painting mode is active, Ctrl+Z undoes the last drawing stroke or
    page insertion/deletion and Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes it; drawing undo is used
    first while drawing history exists.  Undo history keeps 40 steps in every
    mode.  The keys are grabbed only while
    painting, so they keep their normal meaning in other applications.  The
    shortcut also works while Caps Lock or Num Lock is on.
  - The About dialog names the author and email, and states that Screenator is
    a vibe-coded screen annotation app based on Gromit-MPX 1.4.2.
  - Dialogs and message boxes hide the drawings while they are open, so their
    contents are never hidden behind them; the drawings are restored when the
    dialog closes.  This also works in virtual machines without a compositor,
    where the window manager cannot stack a dialog above the fullscreen
    annotation window.
  - A Paper submenu turns the screen into a PDF page for drawing and
    writing.  The page is drawn below the annotations and above the other
    windows, stays when the screen is cleared and is saved with the other menu
    settings.
  - A settings ring around the floating icon shows the line thickness as
    magenta sectors, the line colour in its upper half and the shape fill
    colour in its lower half.  It is on by default and can be turned off with
    "Show Settings Indicator" in Settings.

Build:
  cd screenator
  ./build.sh

  The script downloads and stages the development headers under
  /tmp/opencode/screenator-build-stage (no root required), compiles with CMake,
  builds the .deb, and then builds the AppImage with linuxdeploy, the
  linuxdeploy GTK plugin and appimagetool.  The tools are cached in
  /tmp/opencode/screenator-appimage-tools.

  Both outputs are written to build/ at the project root.  The packages are
  not installed and the GUI is not tested by the build script.
